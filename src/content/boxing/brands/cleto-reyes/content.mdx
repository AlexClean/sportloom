<section id="brand-overview" className="article-section">
<h2 className="article-h2">Cleto Reyes Brand Overview</h2>
<p className="article-p-mt2">Cleto Reyes is a Mexican boxing equipment company whose identity is closely connected to traditional glove making and professional boxing. The story began with founder Cleto Reyes Castro, who worked with leather from a young age and later began improving boxing gloves after his own brief experience in the ring.</p>
<p className="article-p-mt4">The brand’s gloves were approved by the Mexico City Boxing Commission in the 1940s and later became visible in major championship fights. That matters because Cleto Reyes did not become famous by first building a broad lifestyle brand. Its reputation grew around one very specific product category: boxing gloves made for fighters.</p>
<p className="article-p-mt4">Today, the company offers professional fight gloves, training gloves, bag equipment, headgear, body protectors, amateur gear, and other boxing accessories. However, the gloves still define the brand. When most people search for a Cleto Reyes review, they are usually asking whether the gloves justify their premium price.</p>
<p className="article-p-mt4">The simplest way to understand the brand is this: Cleto Reyes represents a traditional Mexican boxing feel. That usually means a punch-focused profile, quality leather, close attention to glove construction, and equipment aimed more at committed boxing use than casual cardio classes.</p>
</section>

<h2 className="article-h2">Cleto Reyes Fit and Sizing: What Buyers Often Get Wrong</h2>
<p className="article-p-mt2">The most common mistake is choosing Cleto Reyes gloves based only on body weight or the number printed on the glove. Ounces describe glove weight, not the exact internal dimensions. Hand shape, wrap thickness, finger length, and the specific model all affect fit.</p>
<p className="article-p-mt4">A new leather glove may also feel firm before it breaks in. That is different from a glove being genuinely too small. <p className="article-p-mt4">Many beginners buy 10 oz or 12 oz gloves because they look compact, then try to use them for every purpose. That may be fine for certain bag or pad sessions, but sparring gyms often require heavier gloves. Always follow your coach’s rules.</p>
